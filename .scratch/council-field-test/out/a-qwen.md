### 1. Experience thesis

At 8/10 only the pending question owns the screen: the question in sans at answer size, options as full-width rows at 8px pitch in a 24px-bounded group, the accent on the one control that continues the run.

The card lands at the head of the reversed feed, directly above the composer. Focus opens on the first option, never the free text; Enter picks, arrows move, digits jump as in the TUI. Beyond six options the card scrolls internally, capped at 18rem like the Code Block. Labels wrap to two lines, never truncate: a truncated answer misrepresents itself.

Copy: opencode's question verbatim as prose; the app adds at most one terse mono line; the header status carries the block.

The moment that should feel good is submit: the card heals to one quiet row, question · answer. Friction to avoid: two answer paths, hover-only affordances, an answered card still outshining the next live row.

### 2. Three highest-value subjective calls

**The card is the only answer path; the composer disables, hinting upward.** Counter: the composer is the answering muscle memory, and a dead composer reads as hijack. You notice when someone types an answer, nothing happens, and the app has taught them it isn't watching.

**A click on an option submits immediately; staged selection plus an explicit continue only for multi-select.** Counter: mixed physics are unpredictable — sometimes a click acts, sometimes it waits — and a mistap into a live process has no undo. People notice because always-staged taxes every trivial yes, and always-immediate makes one slip irreversible.

**Build on the catalogue's Questionnaire, not a bespoke card.** The docs list it unbuilt only because opencode emitted nothing like it; this proposal ages that line, it doesn't license a new row shape (R1). Counter: Questionnaire is a survey form; bending it may be worse provenance than a named exception. People notice in the chrome: a Card inside the transcript panel is the card-inside-a-panel rule 7 rejects.

### 3. The one thing you would cut

The always-visible free-text input. The rare path; a standing box makes the uncommon answer compete with the likely one and costs 375's scarcest space. Keep free text as the group's last row — "type your own" — becoming a field only when chosen, on the 160ms disclosure budget. To decide, I would read transcripts: how often is the answer custom rather than a listed option?
